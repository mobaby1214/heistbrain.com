Average LinkedIn Engagement Rate (2026)

The real LinkedIn engagement rate benchmarks for 2026 — broken down by content type, follower tier, and industry.

The short answer

5.2% of impressions on average — and native documents are the top format. Socialinsider's benchmark study of 1.3 million LinkedIn business-page posts puts the average engagement rate at 5.2% of impressions (2025 data, up 8% year over year). The spread by format is real: native documents average 7.0%, multi-image posts 6.45%, video 6.0%, single images 5.3%, text-only 4.5%, and link posts just 3.25%. Note the definition — this is engagement per impression, not per follower, so don't compare it to Instagram-style rates.

Why LinkedIn engagement looks strong vs other platforms

LinkedIn's 5.2% average looks dramatically higher than X's 0.029% median or Instagram's 0.36% median. Most of that gap is definitional: the LinkedIn figure divides engagement by impressions (people who actually saw the post), while the Rival IQ Instagram and X medians divide by followers (most of whom never saw it).

The practical takeaway: don't compare engagement rates across platforms. Compare your LinkedIn rate to LinkedIn benchmarks and your own history.

The format matters more than anything

In Socialinsider's 2025 data, format is one of the clearest levers you control:

  • Document / carousel posts (7.0% avg): The highest-engagement format. LinkedIn's native document viewer rewards multi-page, save-worthy content.
  • Multi-image posts (6.45% avg) and native video (6.0% avg): Both comfortably above the platform average, and video is growing year over year.
  • Text posts (4.5% avg): The default format sits below the platform average. Plenty of room to grow by rotating in richer formats.
  • Polls (4.2% avg): The old "engagement hack" is fading — poll engagement declined year over year in the same dataset.
  • Link posts (3.25% avg): The weakest format. If the link is the point, consider putting it in the first comment.

Personal profiles beat company pages

The benchmarks above measure company pages. Personal profiles generally out-engage company pages posting the same content — it's why executive content strategy (leaders posting in their own voice and tagging the company) has become standard. Public studies measuring the exact gap are thin, so treat any specific multiplier you see quoted with skepticism, but the direction is well established.

The truncation point matters here too

LinkedIn cuts posts off behind a "see more" link after roughly 210 characters on desktop and about 140 on mobile (AuthoredUp's measurement). Regardless of content type, those first characters are what earn the click — even the best carousel post gets low engagement if its text hook is weak. What moves LinkedIn engagement in 2026

  1. Format rotation. Post carousels 2x/week, text 2x/week, video 1x/week. Variety beats format consistency.
  2. First-hour engagement push. The first 60 minutes determine whether LinkedIn expands your post's distribution. Engage with replies immediately.
  3. Comment quality. LinkedIn weights comments heavily. One-word comments barely count; longer thoughtful replies count a lot.
  4. Posting frequency. 3-5 posts per week is the sweet spot. Above that, self-cannibalization. Below that, algorithm forgets you.
  5. Author bio optimization. Your bio/headline drives follow conversion from post visits. Treat it like ad copy.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good LinkedIn engagement rate in 2026?

Socialinsider's benchmark of 1.3 million company-page posts puts the average at 5.2% of impressions (2025 data). By format: native documents 7.0%, multi-image 6.45%, video 6.0%, single image 5.3%, text 4.5%, link posts 3.25%. Beating the average for your format is a good target.

Why do LinkedIn engagement rates look higher than other platforms?

Mostly definitions. LinkedIn benchmarks typically divide engagement by impressions (people who saw the post), while common Instagram and X benchmarks divide by followers. Rates calculated on different denominators aren't comparable — benchmark within the platform instead.

Should I post on my personal profile or company page?

Personal, almost always. Personal profiles generally out-engage company pages posting the same content, which is why founder and executive posting has become standard practice. Exact multipliers you see quoted are not well measured, but the direction is consistent. Post from the founder/exec profile and tag the company.
