Average YouTube CTR (2026)

The real click-through rate benchmarks for YouTube in 2026, broken down by niche and video length.

The short answer

Half of all channels and videos land between 2% and 10% — that's YouTube's own number. YouTube's click-through rate measures how often your thumbnail earns a click when shown to viewers. Per YouTube's official documentation, half of all channels and videos have an impressions CTR between 2% and 10%, and new videos or channels (or videos with fewer than 100 views) can see an even wider range. There is no credible public per-niche CTR table, and YouTube itself says to compare a video against your own channel's typical CTR rather than a universal target.

One caveat about tables you'll see elsewhere: per-industry CTR breakdowns ("tech reviews average 8-12%") have no published dataset behind them. CTR depends heavily on where impressions come from — a video pushed hard on the homepage will show a lower CTR than one seen mostly by subscribers, even if the thumbnail is better. That's also why comparing CTR across channels is mostly noise.

How YouTube calculates CTR

CTR is calculated as (clicks on your video) / (impressions your thumbnail received). An impression means your thumbnail was shown to a viewer — on the homepage, in search, in suggested videos, or anywhere else. Impression sources matter: YouTube notes that videos with lots of homepage impressions naturally show lower CTR, while videos seen mostly from your channel page show higher CTR.

Why CTR matters so much

CTR is one of the key signals YouTube's recommendation system weighs, alongside watch time. The practical cycle most creators observe:

  1. Upload video. YouTube shows it to an initial audience (heavily your subscribers and engaged fans).
  2. If CTR and retention hold up for your channel and niche, YouTube expands distribution.
  3. If they don't, distribution stalls — regardless of content quality.

This is why thumbnail design matters more than most creators realize: a stronger thumbnail doesn't just win more clicks per impression, it can earn the video more impressions in the first place.

What makes a high-CTR thumbnail in 2026

  • Clear focal point. One face, one object, or one text phrase — not three competing elements.
  • High contrast colors. Bright colors pop against YouTube's dark UI.
  • Expressive faces. Human faces with clear emotion consistently outperform abstract thumbnails.
  • Minimal text (3-5 words max). Text has to be readable at phone-size thumbnails — roughly 200x100 pixels.
  • Curiosity gap. Show enough to interest but not enough to satisfy. The thumbnail answers "what's this about?" while leaving "what happens?" for the video.

The A/B test advantage

YouTube's built-in "Test & compare" feature lets creators upload up to three thumbnail variants and runs them against each other, picking the winner based on watch-time share. Use it. Even a small CTR improvement compounds, because better-clicking videos earn expanded distribution over their lifetime.

Title matters as much as thumbnail

Title and thumbnail work as a pair. A good thumbnail with a weak title still gets low CTR, because the viewer sees both. A/B test them together when possible — or use the subject line tester which applies similar scoring logic to YouTube titles.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good YouTube CTR in 2026?

YouTube's own documentation says half of all channels and videos have an impressions CTR between 2% and 10%, with new videos and channels seeing an even wider range. There's no credible universal 'good' number beyond that — CTR varies with impression sources, so compare each video against your own channel's typical CTR.

How do I improve my YouTube CTR?

Focus on thumbnails first. Use clear focal points, high-contrast colors, expressive faces, and 3-5 words of text maximum. A/B test thumbnails using YouTube's built-in testing feature. Second: improve titles — they're half the CTR equation.

Does CTR matter for YouTube Shorts?

Not in the traditional sense — Shorts measure swipe retention rather than thumbnail CTR. For Shorts, the first 1-2 seconds of video replace the thumbnail as the "hook." Different algorithm, different optimization.
