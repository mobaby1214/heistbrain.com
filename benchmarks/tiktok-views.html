Average TikTok Views in 2026

The real numbers on TikTok views in 2026 — what's average, what's good, what counts as viral.

The short answer

For brand accounts, average views typically land well below follower count — the "10x your followers" rule is a myth. Socialinsider's benchmark of 2 million TikTok videos puts 2025 average views per post at 350 for accounts with 1-5K followers, 3,240 for 10-50K, and 34,900 for 100K-1M — roughly 0.03x to 0.35x of follower count, not 10x. What makes TikTok different is the variance: because distribution runs through the For You feed, a single breakout can massively exceed those averages, while most videos sit near them. Engagement on views is healthy, though: 4.20% of viewers interacted (likes + comments + shares + saves) on average in 2025.

We haven't found a credible public dataset of views by industry, so ignore precise niche-multiplier tables.

Why TikTok views are different from other platforms

On Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, or X, your posts reach a relatively predictable slice of your followers. TikTok doesn't work that way.

TikTok's For You feed distributes content based on content signals — hook retention, watch time, completion — not follower count. This means a 500-follower account can hit a million views on a single video, while a 500K-follower account can get 3,000 views on a weak one.

The "average" is less useful than the distribution

For most TikTok accounts, views follow a heavy-tailed distribution: most videos land near your account's baseline, a few run several times above it, and occasionally one breaks out entirely. Your job isn't to get every video to viral — it's to keep your baseline up while occasionally hitting a breakout that grows your audience.

What "good" looks like for your account

Calculate your 30-day average views per video. That's your baseline. A video that doubles your baseline is strong. A video that 5x your baseline is a potential breakout — study what worked and try to replicate the pattern.

What drives TikTok views in 2026

  1. Hook retention. The first couple of seconds decide whether the algorithm shows your video to more people. Minimize early swipe-aways.
  2. Completion rate. TikTok wants videos that viewers watch to the end — the higher your completion rate, the further your video travels.
  3. Replay / rewatch. Videos that get rewatched are treated as premium content and pushed harder.
  4. Engagement velocity. Likes, comments, shares, and follows in the first hour all matter.
  5. Niche signals. The algorithm needs to understand what your account is about to distribute it correctly. Inconsistent niche = inconsistent views.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good number of views on TikTok?

Depends on your follower count — and the honest baselines are lower than the mythology. Socialinsider's 2-million-video benchmark puts 2025 average views for brand accounts at 350 per post (1-5K followers), 3,240 (10-50K), and 34,900 (100K-1M). Anything well above your tier's average means the algorithm is pushing your content; a breakout can exceed it by orders of magnitude.

Why are my TikTok views so low?

Most common cause: weak hook in the first 2 seconds. The algorithm tests your video with a small sample, and if they scroll past, it doesn't expand. Other causes: inconsistent niche signals (the algorithm doesn't know who to show it to), low completion rate, or poor audio quality.

How many views does the average TikTok get?

Far fewer than the viral clips suggest. In Socialinsider's 2025 brand-account data, a 1-5K-follower account averaged 350 views per video and a 100K-1M account averaged 34,900. Because distribution is so uneven on TikTok, averages are less useful than your own 30-day median.
