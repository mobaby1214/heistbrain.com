Under the Hood

How the Brain actually learns.

Most AI content tools run stateless. Every new chat starts from zero. You re-explain your brand, your audience, your last campaign, your voice — every single time. By week four, you're spending more time prompting than writing.

Heist's Brain works differently. During onboarding, you define seven fields that describe your brand: mission, audience, voice attributes, offer, CTA style, keywords, and tone examples. Those fields get embedded into every generation prompt Heist sends — not pasted on top, embedded. The model sees your voice profile the way it sees the user's current request: as context it must respect.

The feedback loop

Every six hours, Heist pulls engagement data from your connected platforms — likes, comments, shares, click-through rates. Posts that outperform your baseline by a meaningful margin get flagged and the patterns that made them work (hook style, post length, topic angle, emoji use, CTA placement) get saved to your Brain as insight facts. Your next generation sees those facts. Your content gets more signal and less noise over time — not because the AI got smarter, but because your Brain got richer.

Drift detection

Every tenth generation, the Brain runs a drift check. It compares the last ten outputs to your baseline across four dimensions: vocabulary similarity, tonal match, signature-phrase frequency, and structural rhythm. When drift creeps above threshold — usually because the base model's strong prior toward generic prose starts leaking in — the system re-injects your voice anchors and, if drift persists, surfaces it to you directly: "your recent posts are sliding generic, here's what changed."

Why this matters

The promise of AI content is leverage: one hour of thinking turns into a month of posts. That promise breaks the moment your content starts sounding like everyone else's content. A persistent Brain with real engagement data and drift detection is what makes the leverage hold. Without it, you're just renting a very expensive word-shuffler.
